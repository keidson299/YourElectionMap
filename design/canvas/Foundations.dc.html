Two directions for the same search-first page. Same copy, same anatomy — the difference is form.

A · Map-forward

Why: the page shows what the tool does before a word is read. Quiet, cartographic, institutional without being stuffy.

Trade-off: the backdrop competes with the field. It needs the veil and the card to stay legible, and it is the heavier of the two to build.

Copy — identical in both

Tagline: “Enter your address to see your districts, candidates, and upcoming election dates.” · Placeholder: “Enter your home address” · Button: “Find my elections” · “Use my current location” · “We use your address only to look up your voting districts.” · “Nonpartisan · Not affiliated with any election authority”

Accessibility — decided here, not later

Real <label> for the address input, visually hidden. Wrapped in a <form> so Enter submits. Every target ≥44px on mobile. Focus ring contrasts against its own background — the card over map artwork is the risky one.

Build constraints

Plain CSS, no framework or UI library — nothing here needs a new dependency. Map backdrop is inline SVG, not map tiles. Both palettes ship together via prefers-color-scheme, which the client's index.css already declares.
